The problem

What the research looks like without it

Sale lists on one county site. Land use on the appraiser’s site. The case file — deed status, the notice, the liens — buried in the clerk’s portal, formatted differently in every county. Multiply by every county you bid in, every week.

Step 1 · Collected

Every sale, one list

All upcoming tax deed auctions across the covered counties, refreshed automatically. Redeemed sales separated out; foreclosure auctions excluded by design.

Step 2 · Verified

Checked against the records

Owner, land use, acreage and mailing address pulled from the county appraiser; the parcel’s own clerk case file linked with its paperwork — rolling out county by county.

Step 3 · Organized

On one sale calendar

Pick the counties you bid in and every upcoming sale lands on one month view — county, date, start time and how many parcels — printable for the wall.

Every property

The whole file, on one card

Open any property and the research is already laid out: the parcel record, the map, the appraiser link, FEMA flood and national wetlands maps centered on the parcel — and where the county publishes it, the clerk’s case file itself: deed status, the applicant who forced the sale, and every document in the file as a link.

Track your decision right there — interested, watching or pass, your max bid, notes and lists — and it syncs to every device you sign in on.
